The Hook

The Logline

Two broke paranormal vloggers fake ghost hunts for a dying YouTube channel. When they stage one last "investigation" at an abandoned hospital, the dead show up as their livestream audience — and they don't want special effects. They want the truth.

The Premise

The Blurb

Jessica "Jess" Park (28) is the cynical producer of "Ghost Bros," a paranormal investigation channel circling the drain. She's brilliant at what she does — reads analytics like scripture, scripts every jump scare, buys props at thrift stores. She's also a thief: every ghost in their archive is fabricated, every EVP stolen from real 911 calls she never credited.

Marcus Reyes (26) is the earnest heart of the show — the one who cries on camera, who feels cold spots, who genuinely believes in the paranormal despite never experiencing it. He's leaving the channel to become a dental hygienist because "at least mouths are honest."

For their final episode, they break into St. Mary's Hospital. The plan: one fabricated haunting, one viral video, one clean exit. But the hospital has other plans. The dead don't want revenge. They want content — and they'll hold the stream hostage until Jess and Marcus give them the one thing they never faked: the truth.

The face and producer of "Ghost Bros." Cynical, exhausted, and dangerously clever. Jess treats haunting like content strategy — what gets clicks, what gets retention, what gets sponsors. Beneath the armor of irony is a genuinely guilty conscience she's buried so deep she needs a ghost to dig it up. Wears her own merch under a thrifted bomber jacket.
Officer Dunn
Age 50s · The Ghost
A security guard who patrols the hospital. Friendly, out-of-date, offers them coffee from a thermos that pours dust. He mentions his wife, his dog, and the "new patients" with the same cheery tone. Walks through a solid door mid-conversation. The audience notices before the characters do.

Production

Director's Notes

Visual Language

The aesthetic is Studio Ghibli meets indie comic — watercolor-textured flats with soft ink outlines, slightly melancholic, gentle muted palette. This isn't photorealistic horror. The ghosts are warm. The hospital is institutional and faded. The horror lives in implication, not jump scares.

Character Design

All three character reference sheets and 45 storyboard panels were generated via Higgsfield AI using Soul 2.0 for single-character close-ups and GPT Image 2 for multi-character compositions. Character consistency was maintained through Soul IDs and image references.

Sound & Music

The score should feel like a lo-fi hip-hop horror soundtrack — 85-90 BPM, melancholic piano loops with subtle vinyl crackle. No orchestral stings. The ghosts don't need dramatic music. The phone notification ding is the scariest sound in the film.
